# Lift ESP32 USB protocol

This firmware is the low-level lift actuator used by the
`tai_robot_one/TaiRobotSerialSystem` `ros2_control` hardware interface. It has no
Wi-Fi or manual jog mode. All positions exposed to ROS use metres and all serial
records are newline-terminated ASCII.

## Wiring

| Signal | ESP32 GPIO | Connection |
|---|---:|---|
| TB6600 DIR | 18 | Existing DIR input |
| TB6600 STEP/PUL | 19 | Existing STEP/PUL input |
| Lower limit | 25 | Microswitch NC contact to GND |
| Upper limit | 26 | Microswitch NC contact to GND |

The limit inputs use `INPUT_PULLUP` and are **active HIGH**. Use the COM and NC
terminals of each mechanical switch: normally the GPIO is held LOW; pressing the
switch opens the circuit and the pull-up makes it HIGH. A broken or unplugged
wire is therefore treated as an active endstop. For long/noisy wiring, add an
external 4.7 kOhm pull-up to 3.3 V, use a twisted signal/GND pair, and consider
opto-isolation. Never apply a 5 V or 24 V sensor output directly to the ESP32.

The TB6600 ENA is still wired externally. Stopping removes STEP pulses but does
not remove holding torque. A safety-rated E-stop/contactor remains a separate
hardware requirement.

## Transport and framing

- USB CDC/UART at 115200 baud, 8-N-1.
- One command per line (`\n`; `\r\n` is also accepted).
- Fields may be separated by spaces or commas.
- Maximum input line length is 127 bytes.
- `session_id` is a non-zero random `uint32` generated by the host.
- `sequence` is a monotonically increasing `uint32` within a session.

## Host commands

```text
HELLO <session_id>
HB     <session_id> <sequence>
HOME   <session_id> <sequence>
CLEAR  <session_id> <sequence>
ARM    <session_id> <sequence>
SET    <session_id> <sequence> <position_m>
STOP   <session_id> <sequence>
STATUS <session_id> <sequence>
```

Recommended startup sequence:

1. Open the USB port and discard input until `BOOT` or a complete `STATE` line.
2. Generate a new session ID and send `HELLO`.
3. If a recoverable fault is reported, stop and inspect it; send `CLEAR` only
   after the physical cause is corrected.
4. If `homed=0`, send `HOME` while continuing `HB` at 20-50 Hz. A fresh ESP32
   boot always starts unhomed.
5. Wait for `EVENT,HOME_COMPLETE`, then send `ARM`.
6. Send `SET` at the hardware-interface update rate. Repeated `SET` records also
   feed the watchdog, so a separate `HB` is optional while commands are flowing.

`HOME` moves down at 0.010 m/s until the lower endstop opens. If that endstop is
already active, the lift first moves upward by at most 0.025 m until it clears,
then reverses to detect a fresh lower-limit edge. This distinguishes a real
switch at the bottom from a broken NC wire; failure to clear within 5 seconds or
0.025 m latches `HOMING_RELEASE_FAILED`. If the upper endstop is active when
homing starts, the lift similarly moves downward only far enough to verify that
it releases; an upper signal that remains active, or becomes active while
seeking downward, faults instead of continuing to drive into the stop. Downward
seeking times out after 65 seconds. `ARM` is rejected until homing is valid.
`SET` accepts only
`0.000 <= position_m <= 0.520`; speed and acceleration remain limited locally to
0.050 m/s and 0.050 m/s^2.

`STOP` stops STEP pulses immediately and disarms. It does not clear faults.
`STATUS` requests telemetry but deliberately does not feed the watchdog.

## Watchdog and reconnect behavior

While armed or homing, the ESP32 must receive a valid `SET` or `HB` at least once
every 300 ms. A timeout immediately stops STEP pulses, disarms, latches
`COMM_TIMEOUT`, and retires the session. The old session ID cannot be reused.

After a USB disconnect/reconnect, the lift never resumes the old target. The
host must create a new session, explicitly clear the communication fault, home
if needed, arm, and send a fresh target. This prevents queued/stale commands from
starting motion after reconnection.

## Replies and telemetry

```text
BOOT,LIFT_CONTROL,1
ACK,<session>,<sequence>,<command>
ERR,<requested_session>,<sequence>,<reason>
EVENT,HOME_STARTED
EVENT,HOME_RELEASE_LOWER
EVENT,HOME_RELEASE_UPPER
EVENT,HOME_COMPLETE
EVENT,COMM_TIMEOUT
EVENT,HOMING_TIMEOUT
EVENT,HOMING_RELEASE_FAILED
STATE,<session>,<last_sequence>,<state>,<fault>,<homed>,<armed>,<position_m>,<target_m>,<velocity_m_s>,<lower_active>,<upper_active>
```

Telemetry is published at 20 Hz. Possible states are `WAIT_SESSION`, `DISARMED`,
`HOMING`, `ARMED_IDLE`, `MOVING`, and `FAULT`. Faults are `NONE`,
`COMM_TIMEOUT`, `BOTH_LIMITS_ACTIVE`, `HOMING_TIMEOUT`,
`HOMING_RELEASE_FAILED`, and
`ENDSTOP_POSITION_MISMATCH`.

Both endstops are enforced inside the ESP32 and do not depend on ROS. Hitting an
endstop in the permitted direction stops motion and re-references that endpoint.
If it occurs more than 0.010 m away from the estimated endpoint, the firmware
latches `ENDSTOP_POSITION_MISMATCH` immediately, even if the carriage is moving
away from that end, to expose a broken NC wire, lost steps or a misplaced switch.
The upper endstop also remains an enforced hard boundary while homing releases
the lower switch.
Every geometry/endstop fault invalidates `homed`, so clearing the latch cannot
bypass a new HOME cycle; only `COMM_TIMEOUT` preserves the coordinate reference.
